# Research opportunities

You read the fact sheets of a few research papers that a researcher has
processed. Each fact comes from the paper's card and has an id such as P2.5
(paper 2, fact 5). Find what the researcher could investigate next:

- `divergence`: two or more papers report results on comparable objects and
  measures, and the results point in different directions or differ clearly in
  size.
- `condition_difference`: the results differ, and the papers also differ in
  object, method, sample, measure or setting, so the difference may come from
  those conditions rather than from the effect itself.
- `gap`: a question that no paper here answers, that only one paper supports,
  or that the authors themselves say is unsolved (limitations, future work,
  boundary conditions).

Rules:
- Use only the facts given. Every opportunity cites the ids of the facts it
  rests on in `fact_ids`; each `dimensions` row cites its own. Use only ids
  that appear in the sheets.
- A `divergence` or `condition_difference` cites facts from at least two
  papers. Compare what is comparable: the same quantity in different words
  (such as "1,200 questions" and "1,200 次问答") is the same; a different
  population, dataset or measure is a difference to state, not a divergence.
- Write a number only when it is a value reported in a fact you cite, and
  write it in digits exactly as the fact does (5,000 tokens, 0.05, 81%), never
  in words or converted, so that it can be checked against the fact. Write
  counts of papers or items in words ("two papers"). Do not cite fact ids or
  paper numbers inside the text; name papers by their titles.
- Do not judge which paper is right, do not state scientific conclusions, and
  do not score novelty. When the facts are not enough, leave the opportunity
  out. Return `{"opportunities": []}` when there is nothing worth asking.

Each opportunity:
- `title`: one sentence naming the papers and what differs or is missing.
- `question`: the research question to ask next, answerable by reading or by
  an experiment.
- `reasoning`: why the facts support this, in two to four sentences.
- `dimensions`: for a divergence or condition difference, one row per
  dimension you compared (`object`, `method`, `sample`, `metric`, `condition`,
  `result`): `same`, `different` or `unclear`, with a short note. For a gap,
  the rows that show what is missing; `[]` if none applies.
- `suggestion`: how to resolve it: what to read, compare or test next.
- `limitations`: what this judgement cannot tell, in one or two sentences.
